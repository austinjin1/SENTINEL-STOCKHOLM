**Date:** 2026-10-22

## 0. Decision summary

1. **Organism behaviour as a field biomonitor is a dead end on public data.** Daphnia toximeters, Mosselmonitor, MolluSCAN eye, fish ventilation monitors and the Rhine stations all exist and all keep their alarm records private; the one open Rhine station publishes only conductivity/O₂/pH/temperature. There is no labelled contamination-event dataset to train or test on. This is the same wall BioMotion hit, and it should be stated in the paper as the reason the model was withdrawn.
2. **Primary replacement: biological community bioassessment (Track B).** EPA's National Aquatic Resource Surveys give national-scale macroinvertebrate and fish counts **with 25 chemistry analytes measured at the same sites** (NRSA 2018-19: 2,106 invertebrate samples, 1,916 sites, 894 taxa); the UK Environment Agency publishes **280,992 invertebrate samples across 30,735 sites (1965–2026, 3,069 taxa) under OGL v3.0** for anyone wanting enough data to justify a deep architecture; StreamCat supplies 600+ watershed predictors for 2.65M stream reaches; the published comparators are plain random forests and boosted trees (**75% / 73% condition accuracy; 61% of deviance in European WFD status**); and no deep-learning model appears among the 74 works citing the national EPA model. Deep learning has barely been tried here. A blocked baseline is already in hand: **community composition → chemistry, leave-one-ecoregion-out R² 0.29–0.39** (*measured in this session*), against a geography-only control that collapses to 0.06–0.31.
3. **Secondary replacement: chemical structure → aquatic toxicity (Track A), framed as a spill-severity module.** The data and splits are fixed and public (ADORE, CC BY 4.0, verified download), the SOTA has released weights *and* per-chemical held-out errors (TRIDENT, MIT + CC BY), and the field's one unoccupied niche is narrow but real: **no deep-learning regression result exists on ADORE's own recommended splits**, and no regression baseline at all on its cross-taxa challenges.
4. **The headroom is quantified, not assumed.** On ADORE's fish challenge the published best is RMSE 0.927 while the data's own replicate repeatability floor is **0.383 log10 units** (*computed here*) — a factor of ~2.4, so the task is not noise-limited.
5. **The leakage trap is quantified too.** ADORE's own "totally random" split shares **821 chemicals** between train and test; its recommended splits share **zero** (*computed here*). Every number SENTINEL reports must come from the latter.
6. **Optional third modality:** a *laboratory* zebrafish behavioural-bioassay model (~365 environmental chemicals with raw per-animal photomotor traces) — the thing BioMotion should have been — with chemicals and plates held out and a position-randomisation control, never described as in-situ early warning.

Interpretation: on t-F2F the published best (RMSE 0.927) explains roughly 72% of test variance relative to the test SD of 1.756, so the benchmark is real, not a mean-predictor artefact. On **t-A2A the test set is only 85 rows** — any algae-only claim will have enormous error bars.

**Irreducible error (replicate noise), t-F2F, *computed here*:** grouping identical (CAS, species, duration, concentration type, media type, exposure type) experiments gives 13,949 unique experiment keys, 4,083 of which have ≥2 replicates; **pooled within-key SD = 0.383 log10 units** (12,165 dof; mean per-key SD 0.196, median 0.120). Across species/conditions within a chemical (≥5 tests, n=693 chemicals) the SD is median 0.352 / mean 0.415. So the data's own repeatability floor is ≈0.38–0.40 log units while the published model error is ≈0.93 — **there is genuine headroom (~2.4×), the task is not noise-limited.** This is a defensible, quotable framing for "how much better can a model get".

**Leakage check, *computed here*:** in the `split_totallyrandom` column 821 of 1,802 training chemicals also appear in the test set; in `split_occurrence`, `split_random`, `split_scaffold-murcko`, `split_scaffold-generic` and `split_scaffold-murcko-llo` the chemical overlap is exactly **0**. Any SENTINEL result must use the latter.

### A.5 Organism-behaviour biomonitors (the direct "BioMotion" replacement question)

**Finding: there is no public dataset of field/online biomonitor behaviour time series with verified pollution-event labels.** Deployed systems exist (bbe Daphnia Toximeter and Fish Toximeter, Mosselmonitor, MolluSCAN eye, Rhine stations, Korean national network since 1996, ToxMate) but their alarm records are not published. ## (c) Gaps where a SOTA or first-in-class claim is realistic

### C.1 Modality A — the honest claim space is narrow but real

**What is already taken.** As of today the chemical→aquatic-toxicity field is crowded: a transformer SOTA with public weights (TRIDENT, 2024); a unified multitask MMoE model with scaffold/cluster splits, an EC50 ≥ EC10 monotonicity constraint, heteroscedastic uncertainty and head-to-head wins over both TRIDENT and Uni-Mol (Wu et al., *JCIM* 2026); two species-aware deep models with taxonomy and even cytochrome-*b* sequence embeddings (GTGT, *ES&T* 2026; Liu et al., *JHM* 2026); a 50,603-record cross-species benchmark with a 3D model and an anti-leakage library (Yuan et al., *ETC* 2026); applicability-domain geometry (Lin et al., *ES&T* 2026); pairwise/matrix-completion hazard prediction (Posthuma et al., *ES&T* 2025); and ML-predicted SSDs in a regulatory frame (Minnema et al., 2026). **"We built a deep model that predicts aquatic toxicity from structure" is not a 2026 contribution, and "species-aware" is also taken.**

**What is genuinely open (verified gaps).**

1. **No deep-learning *regression* result exists on ADORE's own recommended splits.** A scan of the **68 works citing ADORE** (OpenAlex, 2026-10-22) found exactly one paper reporting regression metrics on an ADORE challenge with ADORE's splits — Gasser et al. 2024, which used LASSO / RF / XGBoost / GP — and one reporting classification (Lou et al. 2025). So "**first deep / pretrained-representation results on ADORE t-F2F, t-C2C, t-A2A under the occurrence and Murcko-scaffold splits, beating the published RF/XGBoost baseline (micro-RMSE 0.927, macro 0.845–0.938)**" is both novel *and* trivially verifiable by a judge: the splits are a column in a public CSV. Low-glamour, high-integrity.
2. **No published regression baseline on the cross-taxa a-CA2F challenges.** Verified composition (*computed here*): `a-CA2F-diff` trains on **7,334 crustacean + algae rows (6,630 + 704; 1,494 chemicals, 63 species)** and tests on **3,576 fish rows (914 chemicals, 71 species) with zero chemical overlap** — double extrapolation to new taxa *and* new chemicals, with a test set large enough for real error bars. Only a classification number exists for it (MACCS-DNN AUC 0.821). A regression result here is **first-in-class on a public benchmark**.
3. **Nobody has reported the noise floor.** The pooled within-experiment replicate SD on t-F2F is **0.383 log10 units** (*computed here*). Framing results as "0.93 against a 0.38 repeatability floor" is a contribution in itself and is exactly the kind of honest accounting the integrity review asked for.
4. **The spill-severity application is open.** No work found that joins a QSAR/DL toxicity model to **geolocated real spill reports** with amount-in-water and receiving-water-body fields. The NRC incident files make this concrete and verifiable (§B.1).

### C.2 Modality A — what is *not* realistic

- Beating TRIDENT's fold errors *overall* on its own 147k-record dataset, in a way that survives scrutiny, within this project's timeline — Wu et al. 2026 already did it with a larger engineering effort, and their numbers are not public for comparison.
- Any claim built on a **random split**. ADORE's own `totally random` column shares 821 chemicals between train and test (*computed here*); this is precisely the error class that sank the previous SENTINEL results.
- Any claim from the **algae-only** challenge: 85 test rows on the occurrence split.

### C.3 Modality B — the broadest open space, and the one that is actually about water quality

1. **Deep learning is essentially untried at national bioassessment scale.** Among the **74 works citing Hill et al. 2017** (OpenAlex, *checked here*) the machine-learning comparators are random forests, spatial regression and explainable-ML variants; the only deep attempt found anywhere in this space is a 2024 Research Square preprint on the Danube with no held-out benchmark metric. So the claim space includes: **"first benchmarked deep model for stream biological condition / assemblage composition under ecoregion-blocked evaluation"**.
2. **The baselines are concrete and the comparison is measurable:** Hill et al. 2017 (OOB accuracy 75%, AUC 0.84, random holdout AUC 0.81), Maloney et al. 2018 (73% on a random 25% holdout), Lemm et al. 2021 (BRT, 61% of deviance in WFD status across >50,000 sub-catchments) — **all under random splits**, plus the **blocked random-forest baseline measured in this session** (community → chemistry, leave-one-ecoregion-out R² 0.29–0.39). SENTINEL must re-run a random forest under its own blocked protocol and report both numbers.
3. **The single best dataset for a *large* deep model is the UK Environment Agency archive, not NRSA**: **280,992 invertebrate samples at 30,735 sites over 1965–2026 with 3,069 taxa and seven per-sample indices, under OGL v3.0**, plus 27,391 diatom samples of which **8,921 are DNA metabarcoding**. That is two orders of magnitude more samples than NRSA and it is the realistic path to a model with enough data to justify a deep architecture — with NRSA/NLA as the independent, different-continent transfer test. (A cross-continent generalisation test is itself a strong, reviewer-pleasing experiment, and it directly extends the transfer-gap finding the integrity review praised.)
4. **Multi-target / joint community prediction is a genuine deep-learning-shaped problem.** Predicting ~100–500 taxa jointly from environment with shared structure (taxon embeddings, residual co-occurrence factors) is exactly where neural JSDMs could beat per-taxon random forests — and Norberg et al. 2019 provides the published evaluation framework for community-level metrics.
5. **The chemistry↔ecology bridge is wide open at US scale.** Moulinec et al. 2025 reached pseudo-R² **0.28** for mixture toxicity → macroinvertebrate MMI at 207 European sites. The same construction on NRSA (chemistry measured at the same sites, thousands of sites, ecoregion-blocked) has no published counterpart.
6. **Reverse direction (biology → stressor) is also open**, and it is the version that supports *diagnosis* ("which stressor is degrading this reach?"), which is more useful to SENTINEL's disaster story than condition scoring alone.

7. **A noise floor exists here too, and it is published:** O/E replicate-sampling precision is SD **0.09–0.11** against a reference-site SD of 0.13–0.18 and null-model SD of 0.20–0.28 (Van Sickle et al. 2005) — so quote results against that floor rather than against perfection.

**What is *not* realistic for modality B:** anything described as early warning or real-time forecasting from NARS alone (one biological visit per site, and NEON taxonomy is published ~210 days after sampling); any result where an index is predicted from the counts it is computed from; and any *random*-split number presented as the headline, because geography alone beats community composition under random CV (§A.4).



### C.4 Modality C (behaviour) — one realistic opening, and it is not a field biomonitor

- **Dead end:** in-situ behavioural early warning (valve gape / Daphnia alarm → contamination event). No public labelled event data exist. The only candidate with real effluent surges plus chemistry (ToxMate) would require a data-sharing agreement with INRAE (Chaumot group). **Do not rebuild BioMotion as a field detector.**
- **Realistic opening:** a **laboratory behavioural-bioassay model** on zebrafish photomotor traces, with **chemicals held out**. Roughly **365 environmental chemicals have raw per-animal traces** (139 PFAS + 61 flame retardants + 104 alkyl-PAHs + 61 EPA chemicals, with some overlap) and ~1,060 more have processed calls. The non-circular task is: **predict 120-h morphology/mortality outcome from the 24-h embryo photomotor trace**, where the label is measured independently of the input (Reif et al. 2016 established that the 24-h signal carries information). "First deep model to predict 120-h teratogenicity from 24-h behaviour with chemicals held out" **may be first-in-class** — this could not be confirmed after the search budget ran out, so treat it as **unverified novelty**.
- **Mandatory guardrails** (both come from published failures): split by **whole chemical and whole plate/run date**, never by larva or well — Gendelev et al. 2024 showed AUROC falling 0.97 → 0.84 when well positions were randomised, i.e. plate layout alone was being learned; and do not score against labels derived from the same statistical test used to define them (the Green et al. 2024 caveat).
- Honest framing: this is **effect-based screening of a chemical or a water sample in the lab**, not in-situ disaster detection, and it must be presented that way.

## (d) Recommended approach

### D.1 The decision

**Replace "BioMotion" with two modalities, not one, and drop organism behaviour as a field modality.**

| Priority | Track | Why | Claim that can honestly be made |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 — primary** | **B: biological community bioassessment** (macroinvertebrates first, then fish/diatoms) | Public data at scale with **co-measured chemistry at the same sites** — NRSA 2018-19 alone gives **2,106 invertebrate samples / 1,916 sites / 894 taxa with 25 analytes**, and the UK EA archive gives **280,992 samples / 3,069 taxa under OGL v3.0**; the published comparators are plain **random forests and boosted trees** (75% / 73% condition accuracy, 61% of deviance); **no deep-learning model appears among the 74 works citing the national EPA model** (*checked here*); and a blocked baseline is already measured (**community → chemistry R² 0.29–0.39 leave-one-ecoregion-out**, *computed here*). It is also the modality most obviously about *water quality*, which is the SJWP framing | "First benchmarked deep model for stream assemblage ↔ chemistry/condition under **ecoregion-blocked** evaluation, beating a random forest re-run under the identical protocol, with a cross-continent (EA → NRSA) transfer test." Realistic. Moderate difficulty |
| **2 — secondary** | **A: chemical structure → aquatic toxicity**, used as a **spill-severity module** | A fixed public benchmark with ready-made leakage-free splits (ADORE), a public SOTA with released weights *and* per-chemical held-out errors (TRIDENT), and a verified real-incident dataset to apply it to (NRC). Cheap: the whole chemical space is 1,905 molecules | "First deep / pretrained-representation regression results on ADORE's recommended splits, beating the benchmark's published RF/XGBoost baseline (micro-RMSE 0.927); first regression baseline on the cross-taxa a-CA2F challenges." Modest but airtight. Low difficulty |
| **3 — optional** | **C: laboratory behavioural bioassay** (zebrafish photomotor) | Real per-animal behavioural time series for ~365 environmental chemicals exist publicly — this is what BioMotion *should* have been | "Predicts 120-h developmental outcome from the 24-h behavioural trace with chemicals held out." Possibly first-in-class (**unverified**). Must never be framed as in-situ early warning |
| **drop** | **C as a field biomonitor** (Daphnia/mussel/fish alarm systems) | **No public dataset with verified pollution-event labels exists** (§A.5). This is the same wall BioMotion hit | none |

**The one-sentence rationale:** modality A is where the *data* are cleanest but the *field is crowded* (TRIDENT 2024, a multitask MMoE model that already beats it in 2026, two species-aware deep models, a 50k-record cross-species benchmark); modality B is where the data are messier but **deep learning has barely been tried**, the baselines are plain random forests, and the output is directly a water-quality statement.

### D.2 Track B design — "BioAssess": community ↔ environment, ecoregion-blocked

**Two non-circular tasks, in this order.**
1. **Environment → biology.** Inputs: StreamCat watershed/landscape metrics + co-measured NRSA water chemistry and physical habitat + climate; target: the **assemblage** (multi-target: relative abundances or presence/absence of the N most common taxa, as a multi-label/compositional head) and, separately, the **condition index**. Comparators: random forest per taxon, RIVPACS/O-E, and the published 73–75% condition-class accuracy — all re-run under the identical blocked protocol.
2. **Biology → stressor** — *start here, because the baseline is already measured.* Inputs: the assemblage (relative abundances aggregated to a fixed taxonomic level, with a total-count offset); targets: log TP, log TN, log conductivity, log chloride, log turbidity, pH, and the Track-A-predicted mixture toxicity. **The number to beat is the blocked random forest in §A.4: R² 0.34 (TP), 0.39 (TN), 0.33 (conductivity), 0.33 (chloride), 0.39 (turbidity), 0.29 (pH) under leave-one-ecoregion-out** — and the geography-only control (0.06–0.31) must appear in the same table. Other comparators: WA-PLS diatom transfer functions (RMSEP 0.21 log10 TP), SPEAR and other trait indices, and Moulinec et al.'s pseudo-R² 0.28 for the toxicity→MMI direction.

**Why a deep model should help here, concretely:** the input is a sparse 449–3,069-dimensional count vector over a taxonomic hierarchy with strong co-occurrence structure, mixed taxonomic resolution and lab-specific identification practice. A taxon-embedding encoder (set transformer / DeepSets over present taxa, with embeddings initialised from the taxonomy and optionally from trait tables) can share strength across related and rare taxa and absorb resolution differences — which is exactly what a per-feature random forest cannot do. That is a mechanism-level argument for the architecture, not an appeal to novelty.

**Protocol (fixed before training).** Primary split: **leave-one-ecoregion-out** (NARS aggregated ecoregions) *and* a spatially blocked variant (≥50 km or by HUC-8); secondary: **leave-one-survey-cycle-out** (train ≤2014, test 2018-19) as an external test. Report the random-CV score alongside purely to quantify the leakage gap. Metrics: multi-target AUC/Tjur R² per taxon plus community-level metrics as in Norberg et al. 2019 for task 1; RMSE/R² plus Spearman for task 2; condition-class accuracy and macro-F1 where the index is the target. Trivial baselines in every table: ecoregion-mean, prevalence-only, and nearest-reference-site.

**Architecture candidates (all small enough that compute is irrelevant here):** a multi-task MLP / set-transformer over environmental covariates with a per-taxon output head and a learned taxon embedding (so rare taxa borrow strength); a deep compositional model with a Dirichlet-multinomial or zero-inflated negative-binomial likelihood (count data, not Gaussian); and a deep JSDM in the sjSDM sense (latent factors for residual co-occurrence) — with sjSDM and HMSC themselves as baselines rather than as the contribution.

### D.3 Track A design — "ChemTox": chemical structure → aquatic toxicity, benchmarked on ADORE

**Benchmark (fixed before any training).**
- Data: ADORE `processed/t-F2F_mortality.csv`, `t-C2C_mortality.csv`, `a-CA2F-same_mortality.csv`, `a-CA2F-diff_mortality.csv`. Target `result_conc1_mean_mol_log` (log10 mol/L); repeat the headline run on the mass-unit target as a robustness check (Gasser et al. found no difference, so a difference would signal a bug).
- Splits: **primary `split_occurrence`** (the authors' recommendation), **secondary `split_scaffold-murcko`** (harder, and it is what the 2026 literature uses). Folds `0–4` for all model selection; rows marked `test` are scored **once**, and the number of configurations tried is reported.
- Report, for every model: micro-RMSE, MAE, R², **macro-RMSE by chemical and by taxon** (Gasser's RMSE_C / RMSE_T, so the numbers are directly comparable), **% of predictions within 1 log unit** (the ECHA/Karamertzanis regulatory yardstick), **median absolute fold error** (so the numbers are comparable to TRIDENT), and bootstrap CIs resampled **by chemical**, not by row.
- Always print in the same table: global-mean, per-species-mean and species+duration-mean baselines (§A.2), the replicate noise floor **0.383**, the published Gasser numbers (0.927 / 0.904 / 0.938 / 0.845), and the chemical-overlap count of each split.

**Models to run (in this order, cheapest first).**
1. **Reproduce the published baseline**: XGBoost + MACCS + the 37 experimental/chemical/taxonomic features. If this does not land near micro-RMSE 0.927, stop and debug — everything downstream depends on it.
2. **TabPFN v2 on fingerprints + descriptors** (fits the 10k-row / 500-feature limit after per-chemical aggregation; strong and nearly hyper-parameter-free).
3. **Chemprop v2 D-MPNN** with the non-structural features as extra FFN inputs (`pip install 'chemprop>=2.2.0'`; no PyG/DGL, so it dodges the broken `torch-cluster`).
4. **CheMeleon foundation initialisation** of the same D-MPNN (`--from-foundation CheMeleon`) — the cheapest pretrained upgrade available.
5. **Frozen ChemBERTa-2 / MoLFormer-XL embeddings + gradient boosting**, then end-to-end fine-tuning if (4) looks promising. A duration- and endpoint-conditioned head, as TRIDENT uses, is the single most important architectural borrow.
6. Optional: `unimol_tools` (3D) if conformer generation for ~2,400 molecules proves cheap.

**Comparison with TRIDENT — do it the careful way.** TRIDENT was trained on ECOTOX + REACH + OpenFoodTox, so **its training chemicals overlap ADORE**; running the public HF weights over ADORE's test rows would be contaminated and must not be reported as a head-to-head. Instead use `03_Model_error_per_SMILES_from_10x10_CV.xlsx`, which gives TRIDENT's **held-out** per-SMILES L1 error for all nine model variants, and compare on the intersection of chemicals, paired, with a sign test. State the units carefully: TRIDENT works in log10 mg/L, ADORE's molar target in log10 mol/L.

**Expected difficulty: low-to-moderate.** The chemical space is 1,905 molecules; a GINE training step on 128 molecules took 0.685 s and 21 MB of VRAM here (§B.2). The whole track is days of work, dominated by careful bookkeeping rather than compute. **Expected outcome:** a 5–15% RMSE improvement over 0.927 is plausible; a *failure* to beat it is itself reportable (see §E.2).

### D.4 Track A application — "spill severity", the disaster tie-in

This is where modality A earns its place in SENTINEL rather than being a generic QSAR exercise.

1. Parse NRC annual incident files (`https://nrc.uscg.mil/FOIAFiles/CY{YY}.xlsx`, ~15 MB/yr, 1990–present; dictionary verified in §B.1). Keep records with `If Reached Water = Yes`, a usable `CAS Number`, `Amount In Water` + units, `Body of Water`, and lat/long.
2. Map CAS → structure (DSSTox/DTXSID via the ECOTOX `validation/chemicals.txt` or CompTox) → predict per-taxon EC50/EC10 with the Track-A model → build a **model-based species sensitivity distribution** and take HC5.
3. Combine with release mass and a transparent dilution assumption (receiving-water discharge from the nearest USGS gauge where one can be matched) to produce a **risk-quotient severity score** per incident, with the model's own uncertainty propagated. *Verified this session:* the USGS NWIS services need no key — `https://waterservices.usgs.gov/nwis/iv/?format=json&sites=01646500&parameterCd=00060&period=P1D` returns instantaneous discharge, and `https://waterservices.usgs.gov/nwis/site/?format=rdb&stateCd=md&siteType=ST&hasDataTypeCd=iv&parameterCd=00060` enumerates gauges for nearest-gauge matching.
4. Validate what *can* be validated and say so plainly: NRC's own `Water Supply Contaminated` flag, `Waterway Corridor Closed` and evacuation/damage fields give weak, reporting-biased labels; an honest evaluation is "does the severity score rank incidents consistently with the response actions recorded", **not** "predicts fish kills". If a state fish-kill registry can be obtained (**availability unverified**), that becomes the proper label.
5. Fusion role: the output is a **chemical-hazard channel** keyed by (time, lat/lon, waterbody) — exactly the shape the fusion layer needs, and it is independent of the sensor and imagery channels, so it adds information rather than correlated noise.

### D.5 How the two tracks enter multimodal fusion and disaster prediction

| Channel | Key | What it contributes | Disaster class served |
|---|---|---|---|
| Track A, "spill severity" | (time, lat/lon, water body, CAS) | predicted per-taxon EC50/EC10 → model-based SSD → HC5 → risk quotient given released mass and gauged discharge | **chemical spills** and, via pesticide/pharmaceutical loadings, chronic chemical pressure |
| Track B, community condition | (site, visit) → NHDPlus COMID / HUC | the **ecological-response** channel: what actually lives there, and the inferred stressor profile | **ecological degradation, fish kills, pesticide/salinity events**; also the ground truth for "did the predicted pressure matter?" |
| Existing SENTINEL sensor/remote channels | (time, lat/lon) | continuous physico-chemical and optical signals | HABs, hypoxia, sediment |

Two concrete fusion designs that do not require new data collection:

1. **Co-located ecological-condition fusion.** NRSA/NLA sites carry their own co-measured water chemistry and physical habitat, sit on NHDPlus reaches with StreamCat watershed metrics, and **59.7% of a random sample of 119 NRSA 2018-19 sites have at least one USGS NWIS site within 10 km up- or downstream on the main stem** (NLDI API, *measured here*) — so a gauge/sensor channel is attachable for roughly six sites in ten. NEON's 34 aquatic sites add repeat biological sampling alongside 1-minute sondes, 15-minute nitrate and continuous discharge, with a token-free sensor route via the EFI NEON4cast targets file. A fusion model whose inputs are (sensor/chemistry time series + watershed features + predicted chemical hazard from Track A) and whose target is the **biological response** is a genuinely multimodal, non-circular setup — and the *honest* plug-and-play test is whether adding each channel improves an ecoregion-blocked score over the best single channel.
2. **Reproduce and beat the published A↔B bridge.** Moulinec et al. 2025 regressed macroinvertebrate MMI on TRIDENT-predicted mixture toxicity at 207 sites and reached **pseudo-R² 0.28**. The same construction is possible at US scale with NRSA chemistry + Track A predictions + NRSA benthic MMI, with ecoregion-blocked CV. Beating 0.28 (on comparable data, with the trivial baselines reported) is a modest, verifiable and *novel* claim, and it is exactly the kind of chemistry→ecology linkage the SJWP framing rewards.

### D.6 Track C (behaviour), if a third modality is wanted

Only as a **laboratory bioassay** model: zebrafish 24-h embryo photomotor trace → 120-h morphology/mortality, chemicals and plates held out, position-randomisation control reported, baselines = per-interval-feature GBM and the standard KS/AUC statistical test, comparison point = Green et al. 2024 (AUROC 0.740–0.922 on 30 chemicals, with its label-circularity caveat). Pretraining on the UCSF 12,000-molecule library and fine-tuning on the ~365 environmental chemicals is a credible extra. **Never** describe it as in-situ early warning.

## (e) Risks and fallbacks

### E.1 Risks that already sank the previous version of this work

| Risk | How it shows up here | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| **Random-split leakage** | ADORE's `totally random` column shares **821 chemicals** between train and test (*computed here*); CV RMSE drops from ~1.0 to ~0.50 purely from that. The ACS Omega AUCs of 0.98 are most plausibly explained the same way | Use only `split_occurrence` and `split_scaffold-murcko` / `-generic`; publish the chemical-overlap count (0) for every split used |
| **Circular labels** | The retracted ToxiGene/GNN findings used a label that was a threshold of its own input. In behaviour work, Green et al. 2024's labels came from the same statistical test used for scoring | Only use labels measured independently of the inputs (e.g. 24-h behaviour → 120-h morphology; chemistry → *next* season's biology) |
| **Best-of-N on the test set** | The review found ToxiGene's LOSO 0.68 was the best of ≥26 configurations scored on the same folds | Model selection **only** on ADORE's CV folds 0–4; the `test` rows are touched exactly once, and the number of configurations tried is reported |
| **Missing trivial baselines** | Fig 7's fabricated deltas | Report the global-mean / per-species-mean / species+duration-mean RMSEs from §A.2 in every table, plus the replicate noise floor (0.383) |
| **Hard-coded figure values** | Fig 7 literals in `paper_figures_v3.py` | Every figure value must be read from a results JSON/CSV emitted by the run that produced it |
| **Shortcut features** | Gendelev et al. 2024: plate layout alone gave AUROC 0.97 → 0.84 after randomisation | Block by plate/run date; run a position-randomisation control and report it |
| **Misattributed headline numbers** | "BioMotion 0.85" was gradient boosting on metadata | Name the model that produced each number in the table itself; keep the GBM/RF baselines in the table rather than deleting them (commit `da5eb11` removed baselines that beat the model) |

### E.2 Modality-specific risks and fallbacks

**A (chemistry → toxicity).**
- *Risk:* the claim is incremental and a judge asks "so what?" → *Fallback:* lead with the cross-taxa `a-CA2F-diff` result (first regression baseline, genuine double extrapolation) and the spill-severity application, not with t-F2F.
- *Risk:* the pretrained models underperform the published XGBoost (very possible — AttentiveFP scores R² 0.666 on a 956-chemical fish set where a multitask descriptor model reaches 0.789). *Fallback:* that is a publishable negative result **if** the trivial baselines, the noise floor and the tuning budget are reported; it is also why CheMeleon/Chemprop-v2 and TabPFN-v2-on-fingerprints should both be run — one of them beating 0.927 is likely, and an honest "pretrained graph models do not beat XGBoost on ADORE" is a real finding consistent with Liu et al. 2025's tabular-data argument.
- *Risk:* licensing/token friction (TabPFN ≥2.5 weights are non-commercial and may need `TABPFN_TOKEN`). *Fallback:* pin TabPFN **v2**.
- *Risk:* `torch-cluster` is broken in this environment (GLIBC 2.32). *Fallback:* stay with Chemprop v2 (no PyG/DGL), ChemBERTa/MoLFormer via `transformers`, and `unimol_tools`; avoid MolCLR/GROVER/MiniMol unless time is abundant.

**C (behaviour).**
- *Risk:* presenting a lab photomotor model as a field early-warning system — exactly the overclaim that was retracted. *Fallback:* frame it as effect-based *screening*, and say in the paper that no public field biomonitor data exist.
- *Risk:* the Tanguay GitHub data carry **no license**. *Fallback:* use the Figshare alkyl-PAH release (CC BY 4.0) and the EPA ScienceHub release as the primary sources, and email the lab for permission on the rest.

### E.3 Track B (bioassessment) — the specific traps

| Trap | Why it bites | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| **Index circularity** | NRSA's `BENT_MMI` / condition class is a **deterministic function of the very taxa counts** in the companion file. "Predict MMI from the macroinvertebrate counts" is arithmetic dressed as machine learning — the same class of error as the retracted GNN whose label was a threshold of its own input | Predict **biology from environment** (land use, hydrology, chemistry, climate), or **chemistry/stressor levels from biology** — never the index from its own inputs. If the index is the target, the counts must not be features |
| **Reference-site chemistry** | Reference-condition screens and MMI calibration use chemistry and habitat variables, so some "environmental predictors" partly encode the label | Document which screening variables were used by the survey and exclude them, or report a sensitivity analysis with them removed |
| **StreamCat "special metrics"** | StreamCat ships a *predicted biological condition* layer (the Hill et al. 2017 output). Using it as a feature means predicting the label from someone else's prediction of the label | Hard-exclude those columns; grep the feature list before training |
| **Spatial autocorrelation** | Neighbouring sites share watersheds and ecoregions; random CV over sites massively inflates scores. The review already caught this pattern in SENTINEL (microcystin 0.82 → 0.755 under 5-km blocking; FIB date/geography leakage) | Primary protocol must be **ecoregion-blocked (leave-one-ecoregion-out) or spatially blocked (HUC/≥50 km) CV**, with a random-CV number shown alongside purely to quantify the gap — and the gap reported as a finding, as the review praised in the transfer-gap memo |
| **Temporal / survey-cycle confounding** | NRSA runs in cycles with changing protocols and labs; models can learn the cycle | Hold out a whole survey cycle as an external test (e.g. train ≤2014, test 2018-19) and report it |
| **Taxonomic resolution and lab effects** | Counts depend on the identifying lab, target subsample size (e.g. 300-organism fixed counts) and taxonomic resolution; these differ across surveys and states | Aggregate to a fixed taxonomic level (family or a standardised OTU list), use relative abundances plus a total-count offset, and never mix surveys without a per-survey indicator — then show the model is not just reading that indicator |
| **One visit per site** | Most NRSA sites have a single biological visit, so there is no within-site temporal signal; "early warning" claims are impossible from this data alone. NEON's taxonomy is published **~210 days** after sampling, so even NEON cannot support real-time alarms | Frame Track B as **condition/stressor inference and co-located fusion**, not forecasting. Repeat structure does exist and should be used: NRSA 2018-19 has **188 second visits**, and **586 UNIQUE_IDs recur between 2018-19 and 2023-24** |
| **Geography masquerading as biology** | *Measured here*: under random CV, latitude/longitude/elevation alone give higher R² than the whole 449-taxon community for 5 of 6 chemistry targets (e.g. TP 0.50 vs 0.43). A random-split "community predicts chemistry" headline would be a map lookup | Always report the **geography-only control** in the same table, and make the blocked number the headline |
| **Access and licence changes mid-project** | **NEON now returns HTTP 403 without a token and moved from CC0 to CC BY 4.0 on 2026-06-30**; USGS BioData is **permanently retired**; the WQP legacy profiles are missing USGS data added after 2024-03-11 | Use the token-free `neonDivData` mirror and the EFI NEON4cast targets file for sensor data; obtain a NEON token early if raw NEON products are needed; record the download date and release tag of every file |
| **HAB-label circularity** | Predicting microcystin from NLA phytoplankton counts is partly circular, because cyanobacterial taxa are themselves the features | If the target is a toxin, exclude the producing taxa from the inputs, or predict the toxin from *environment* instead and use the taxa only as an auxiliary target |

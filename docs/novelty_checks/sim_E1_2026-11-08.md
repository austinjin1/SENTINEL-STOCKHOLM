# Novelty re-check: E1, diel-resolved warming sensitivity of DO (solubility vs metabolism), 2026-11-08

**Claim checked (sim_protocol.md §9 E1).** At USGS continuous-DO sites (JJA), per-site anomaly regressions of
DO_min and DO_mean on T_w. Each slope is compared with the pure-solubility slope β_sol, giving a metabolic
remainder β_bio. Hypotheses: H-E1a (DO_min more warming-sensitive than DO_mean) and H-E1b (median β_bio,min < 0).
This extends sim_E1_2026-10-25.md.

## Effect on novelty wording
The wording must be **softened in one respect**; the classification "partially novel (extension)" is
**unchanged**.

Prior work now covers several pieces:
- a global solubility-vs-metabolism-vs-heatwave attribution (Guan et al. 2026, metabolism ≈ 12 %);
- ML attribution of global hypoxia increases (Graham et al. 2025);
- nighttime dominance of hypoxia in high-frequency data (Blaszczak et al. 2023; Shen & Zhi EGU26 abstract,
  withdrawn [U]).

So E1 must not present "separating solubility from metabolism" as new, even in the diel framing. The permitted
wording becomes: "Prior global analyses partitioned deoxygenation into solubility and metabolic components from
grab or satellite-derived data (Guan et al. 2026; Graham et al. 2025; Zhi et al. 2023). We provide, to our
knowledge, the first sensor-based, site-by-site estimate of how nightly-minimum DO responds to temperature
relative to the pure-solubility expectation." The diel and DO_min focus (H-E1a) remains the novel element.

**Scoop risk:** the Zhi group's sensor/nighttime line (EGU26-1567) means a true Scholar forward-citation check of
Zhi 2023 NCC, Blaszczak 2023 and Guan 2026 is required immediately before paper text.

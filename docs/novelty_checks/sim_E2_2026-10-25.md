# Novelty and literature check: E2, upstream–downstream propagation of WQ signals, 2026-10-25

**Scope (docs/research/06 §E2).** For NLDI main-stem pairs of USGS continuous sensors (temperature, SpC,
turbidity):
- estimate lag, gain and attenuation of pulses by cross-correlation, transfer entropy or PCMCI+;
- regress them on channel distance, NHDPlus travel time, ΔQ and dams;
- quantify the *early-warning lead time* an upstream sensor buys;
- use reverse-direction and disconnected-pair negative controls.

**Method caveat.** Searches used a general web index, not Scholar or WoS. A forward-citation check of Sendrowski &
Passalacqua 2017 could not be run as a citation query.

## Searches (web, 2026-10-25)
1. "upstream downstream water quality sensor lag travel time specific conductance turbidity propagation USGS
   network early warning lead time" (extended).
   - **Gamakumara, Santos-Fernandez, Talagala, Hyndman, Mengersen & Leigh 2023, arXiv:2305.12651**, "Conditional
     normalization in time series analysis". GAM-based conditional cross-correlation used to *estimate water
     travel time between two river locations* from upstream and downstream WQ sensors (turbidity, with level and
     temperature as covariates).
     - The case study sites are not stated on the abstract page [U]; we believe they are in Queensland,
       Australia [U].
     - This is a methodological precedent for lag estimation from WQ sensors, on one or a few pairs, not national.
   - Otherwise only USGS real-time WQ program pages and sensor-comparison reports were found.
2. "Sendrowski Passalacqua 2017 transfer entropy Wax Lake Delta …". Sendrowski & Passalacqua 2017, "Process
   connectivity in a naturally prograding river delta" (March 2017; WRR per docs/research/06 [V there]; DOI not
   seen here [U]).
   - Transfer entropy between discharge, tides and wind and water levels at 5 islands + 1 channel.
   - Follow-up: Sendrowski et al. 2018, *Entropy* 20(1):58, doi:10.3390/e20010058, which uses TE to validate
     Delft3D.
   - An AGU 2015 abstract mentions turbidity and temperature records but analysed water levels.
   - **No WQ-signal propagation was found in this line of work.**
3. "cross-correlation lag stream temperature upstream downstream gauge pairs travel time national scale".
   - R package **hydroroute** `get_lag` (CRAN): CCF-peak lag between adjacent gauges, after Greimel et al. 2022
     (hydropeaking).
   - A 2019 WCU MSc thesis (daily data hide sub-daily lags).
   - Not WQ, not national.
4. "graph neural network river network water quality forecasting upstream stations … SpC turbidity USGS".
   - USGS Fair-Graph (AAAI 2024; pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70253018): Delaware River Basin *temperature*.
   - An IMSA 2024 student GNN poster (Maumee).
   - "The Merit of River Network Topology for Neural Flood Forecasting" (arXiv:2405.19836; authors not recorded
     [U]): network topology did *not* help discharge in LamaH-CE.
   - No SpC or turbidity GNN with upstream benefit quantified.
5. "information flow OR transfer entropy river network water temperature conductance sensors upstream
   downstream travel time".
   - Neri, Coulibaly & Toth 2022 (J. Hydrol.): TE from forcing to streamflow at more than 200 Austrian catchments.
   - EGU06 abstract: river–groundwater travel time by correlation shift.
   - No river-network WQ TE study.
6. "longitudinal propagation specific conductance pulse road salt downstream multiple USGS gages lag attenuation".
   No network lag-attenuation study. Hits: an in-network lake damps salinity (Upper Yahara, par.nsf.gov 10415152)
   and Maryland road-salt reports.
7. "river water quality early warning monitoring station placement travel time upstream sensor lead time spill".
   - Waterloo thesis (Grand River, single-station design trade-off; about 11 km upstream optimum).
   - USGS NJ EWS project (time-of-travel studies used to site stations).
   - Russian River EWS, Delaware Valley EWS.
   - Practice exists at single-system scale; we found no national empirical lead-time analysis.
8. "stream temperature signal propagation downstream sensor network lag distance … continental" (2024).
   - Beaufort et al. 2016 (T-NET, Loire, 128 stations: a model, not lag estimation).
   - Riverscape riparian-thinning propagation (USGS 70224956; 100 to >1000 m).
   - USGS 2024 annual-signal transport simulation (J. Hydrol. S0022169424007868; Hare/Briggs line).
   - UC Davis "aquatic heatwave propagation, western US" (active project, no results).
9. Seen in the causal check: **Yu, Li & Qu, EGU26-21949**, "Bridging prediction and causal attribution in
   large-scale river WQ networks". Topology-aware causal forecasting with *travel-time-dependent delays* on
   China's national network of more than 1,900 stations. Abstract only; no metrics.
   **This is the closest national-scale analogue**, but it is a forecasting model, not an empirical lag-vs-travel-time
   analysis, and it is in China, not the USGS network.

## Conclusion (provisional)
- **Precedents exist** for:
  - lag/travel-time estimation from paired WQ sensors on a few pairs (Gamakumara 2023);
  - TE timescales that match physical travel times in one delta (Sendrowski & Passalacqua 2017);
  - network-aware WQ forecasting at national scale in China (EGU26-21949, abstract).
- **Not found:**
  - a *national, empirical* census of USGS sensor pairs relating estimated WQ-pulse lag and attenuation
    (T, SpC, turbidity) to NHDPlus travel time, distance, tributary dilution and dams;
  - an estimate of *usable early-warning lead time* by river size;
  - reverse-direction or disconnected-pair negative controls.
- Classification: **partially novel (extension)**. The method components are established; the novelty is scale,
  multi-constituent comparison and the lead-time framing.
- Permitted wording: "We present, to our knowledge, the first national-scale empirical analysis of WQ-signal
  propagation between paired USGS sensors, validated against computed travel times."
- Avoid "first use of transfer entropy/PCMCI for travel time".
- Cite Sendrowski & Passalacqua 2017, Gamakumara et al. 2023 and EGU26-21949.

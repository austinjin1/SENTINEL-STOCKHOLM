# Novelty and literature check: E3, groundwater/thermal-buffering class as a control on DO resilience, 2026-10-25

**Scope (docs/research/06 §E3).**
- Classify sites with the Hare et al. 2021 paired air–water annual-signal metrics, recomputing them where Hare does
  not cover a site and validating against Hare's classes.
- Test whether shallow-groundwater sites show larger warming sensitivity of T and DO_min (E1 slopes) and more
  hypoxia than deep-groundwater sites. Exclude dam-class sites.

**Method caveat.** Searches used a general web index. Appendix B asks for "forward citations of Hare 2021 that
mention dissolved oxygen", which could not be run as a citation query and was approximated by topical searches.

## Hare et al. 2021: verified classification method
**Source:** Hare, Helton, Johnson, Lane & Briggs 2021, *Nat. Commun.* 12:1450, doi:10.1038/s41467-021-21651-0.
Full text read at https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7933412/ (2026-10-25).

**Signal fit.**
- Daily-mean air T (NOAA station within 25 km) and stream T are each fitted with a static sinusoid,
  α sin(t) + β cos(t) + C (scipy curve_fit, RMSE).
- A = √(α²+β²) and ϕ = arctan(β/α).
- A_r = A_stream / A_air. Δϕ = ϕ_stream − ϕ_air, in days.
- Data rules: stream T < 1 °C excluded; ≥ 2 consecutive years in 2010 or later; no gaps ≥ 30 days.

**Major dam class.** Quote: "We assigned sites that either had phase lags of greater than 40 days … or are within
25 km downstream of major dams, as sites with major dam signatures." Also: "All A r values less than 0.4 were
manually checked for a major dam within 30 km upstream." 305 of the 1,729 sites met the dam criterion.

**Thresholds.** Quote: "We categorized sites as having an atmospheric, shallow groundwater, or deep groundwater
signature by identifying 'conservative' threshold values of A r (0.65) and Δϕ (10 days)."
- **Shallow groundwater:** Δϕ ≥ 10 d (and ≤ 40 d, otherwise dam). Quote: "we used the threshold phase lag of 10
  days to identify sites with a shallow groundwater signature". Whether the bound is > or ≥ 10 is [U].
- **Deep groundwater:** A_r < 0.65 with lag below 10 d. Quote: "Sites with deep groundwater signatures had an A r of
  0.05 to 0.65". The precedence when A_r < 0.65 *and* Δϕ ≥ 10 d is not stated explicitly in the text; we infer that
  shallow takes priority [U]. Check Supplementary Fig. 1 or the code.
- **Atmospheric:** Quote: "Sites with atmospheric signatures in our dataset had an A r between 0.65 and 1.1".
  Δϕ < 10 d is implied.
- A_r > 1.1 removed. Δϕ < −10 d dropped (n = 25); Δϕ between 0 and −10 d set to 0.
- The authors note A_r up to about 0.75 may still indicate deep groundwater "with less certainty".

**Class statistics.**

| Class | n | Mean A_r (σ) | Mean Δϕ (σ) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Deep | 264 | 0.54 (0.10) | 3.8 d (3.4) |
| Shallow | 292 | 0.59 (0.18) | 16.6 d (6.6) |
| Atmospheric | 868 | — | — |

556 of the 1,424 non-dam sites (39 %) carry a groundwater signature.

**Code.** Zenodo doi:10.5281/zenodo.4313244 (GitHub Haredkb/PairedAir-StreamAnnualTSignals). Also the USGS PASTA
tool (pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70243009), which does paired air–stream analysis for groundwater influence.

## Searches (web, 2026-10-25)
1. "Hare Helton … Continental-scale analysis of shallow and deep groundwater contributions to streams". Confirmed
   the citation above. Abstract: shallow-groundwater sites show "reduced baseflow and a higher proportion of warming
   trends". **No DO analysis in Hare 2021.**
2. "groundwater signature Hare 2021 paired air stream temperature dissolved oxygen hypoxia … resilience" (extended).
   - No paper linking Hare classes to DO was found.
   - Sergeant & Falke 2023 ("Hypoxia vulnerability in the salmon watersheds of Southeast Alaska", STOTEN per search
     listing [U]; data doi:10.5066/P9VAKF20): hypoxia vulnerability with low-gradient reaches most prone. Regional,
     and groundwater classes are not used [U].
   - USGS 2024 "Taking heat (downstream)" (J. Hydrol. S0022169424007868): simulates annual-signal transport. T only.
3. "groundwater discharge stream dissolved oxygen low oxygen groundwater inflow summer DO minimum thermal refuge".
   **Important counter-evidence:**
   - **HESS 22:6383, 2018** (hess.copernicus.org/articles/22/6383/2018; first author believed to be Briggs [U]):
     seepage zones in open-valley reaches on Cape Cod are mostly **suboxic**; brook trout redds sit at
     higher-DO seeps. Thermal surveys alone do not indicate habitat suitability.
   - Kurylyk et al. 2015 (Ecohydrology; dalspace PDF, thermal refugia review; attribution of this specific point [U]):
     anoxic groundwater discharge can make cold anomalies ineffective refugia.
   - **Groundwater can lower DO directly**, so "groundwater buffering = DO resilience" is *not* a safe prior.
4. "stream thermal sensitivity groundwater index predicts DO saturation or hypoxia across many sites".
   - Snyder et al. 2015 (Ecol. Appl.; 78 Shenandoah sites, T only).
   - Morrill, Bales & Conklin 2005 (J. Environ. Eng.; air–stream T slopes ≈ 0.6–0.8; DO concern stated
     qualitatively).
   - PASTA.
   - No multi-site test of groundwater index → DO was found.
5. Seen in E1: Blaszczak et al. 2023 (hypoxia more likely in warmer, smaller, low-gradient, urban/wetland rivers;
   no groundwater-class predictor reported in the abstract).

## Conclusion (provisional)
- Hare 2021 classes are established for *temperature* (warming trends, baseflow).
- We found **no study using Hare-type groundwater signatures to explain DO_min sensitivity or hypoxia** across
  many sites.
- Classification: **partially novel (combination/extension)**, consistent with docs/research/06 ("Medium–High as
  a combination").
- The hypothesis must be **two-sided**:
  - groundwater damps warming (protective for DO_min via solubility and ER);
  - but groundwater can be suboxic (Briggs 2018), which may *lower* DO at deep-groundwater sites.
- Pre-register both directions. Report DO% saturation alongside mg/L.
- Permitted wording: "To our knowledge, groundwater signature classes (Hare et al. 2021) have not been tested as
  predictors of diel DO sensitivity or hypoxia at continental scale."
- Do not say "groundwater buffers DO", which is a claim about the result.

# Novelty and literature check: E1, diel-resolved warming sensitivity of DO, 2026-10-25

**Scope (docs/research/06 §E1).** At about 400–500 USGS continuous-DO sites (plus NEON), estimate within-season
detrended sensitivities of DO_min (nightly), DO_mean and saturation deficit D to water temperature. Partition
the result into solubility (Benson–Krause) and metabolism/gas-exchange components, and explain departures with
catchment traits.

**Method caveat.** Searches used a general web index, not Scholar or WoS. The Appendix B forward-citation checks
(Zhi 2023, Blaszczak 2023) could not be run as true citation queries. Below they are approximated by topical
2024–2026 searches. This remains a gap.

## Searches (web, 2026-10-25)
1. "dissolved oxygen nightly minimum warming temperature sensitivity rivers continental high-frequency sensor
   solubility metabolism" (extended). Hits:
   - **Zhi, Ouyang, Shen & Li 2023, Nature Water, doi:10.1038/s44221-023-00038-z.** "Temperature outweighs light
     and flow as the predominant driver of DO in US rivers". The full author manuscript was read at
     osti.gov/pages/servlets/purl/2458192.
     - LSTM on "sparse water quality (grab samples) data from 580 rivers".
     - The authors state that "the coarse data here limit finer scale analysis at hourly or diel scale", and that
       with high-frequency sensors "drivers of DO dynamics at hourly to diel scales can be explored".
     - This is an explicit gap statement that E1 targets.
   - Fu River 2025 (PubMed 41274267). Minimum *air* temperature drives DO changes. This is a regional
     monitoring-station study, not diel sensors.
   - Rajwa-Kuligiewicz et al. (lowland rivers, 10-min data). A few sites only.
2. "Zhi 2023 Widespread deoxygenation in warming rivers … high-frequency sensor nocturnal". Confirmed **Zhi,
   Klingler, Liu & Li 2023, Nat. Clim. Change 13:1105–1113, doi:10.1038/s41558-023-01793-3**.
   - 580 US + 216 European rivers; daytime grab samples.
   - The authors say the rates "may be underestimated" because of daytime sampling.
   - No sensor or nocturnal analysis.
3. "Blaszczak 2023 Extent, patterns, and drivers of hypoxia …". **Blaszczak et al. 2023, Limnol. Oceanogr. Lett.
   8(3):453–463, doi:10.1002/lol2.10297.**
   - 118 M DO–T pairs at more than 125,000 locations.
   - Hypoxia (< 2 mg/L) at 12.6 % of sites.
   - High-frequency data show a "3-h median duration" for hypoxic events, "most likely to initiate at night".
   - Drivers are warmer, smaller, lower-gradient, urban or wetland rivers.
   - It covers occurrence and drivers, **not** temperature sensitivity or a solubility/metabolism split.
4. "dissolved oxygen diel amplitude warming trend USGS continuous monitoring national … saturation deficit"
   (extended). New hit:
   - **Guan, Shi et al. 2026, Science Advances, doi:10.1126/sciadv.aef3132**, "Sustained deoxygenation in global
     flowing waters under climate warming". Read via press coverage only (openaccessgovernment.org, WAM,
     english.cas.cn); the paper itself returned 403.
     - ML stacking on 21,439 reaches, 1985–2023; −0.045 mg/L per decade; 79 % of reaches deoxygenating.
     - Attribution: **solubility ≈ 62.7 %**, heatwaves ≈ 22.7 %.
     - Data type and diel resolution are unknown [U]; they are probably Landsat/grab-based (one news item says
       "Landsat-based") [U].
     - **This already reports a solubility-vs-other attribution at global scale. E1 must position against it.**
5. "river deoxygenation high-frequency sensor data diel nighttime DO declines … 2025 2026" (extended). No
   national sensor-based diel study was found.
6. "river hypoxia frequency hours temperature low flow continuous USGS … after Blaszczak". Carter, Blaszczak,
   Heffernan & Bernhardt 2021, L&O 66:2251–2265, doi:10.1002/lno.11751. North Carolina Piedmont; point
   measurements are "highly problematic" for hypoxia; one river. Supports the diel framing, but is not national.
7. "heatwave river dissolved oxygen high-frequency USGS … Tassone 2023". Tassone et al. 2023 (L&O Letters;
   doaj 308d59f7…) cover temperature heatwaves at 70 USGS sites with no DO analysis. Useful as the event list for
   E1 validation (iv).
8. "daily minimum dissolved oxygen temperature sensitivity hundreds of streams continuous sensors metabolism
   versus solubility" (extended). No relevant multi-site study.
9. "DO percent saturation trends versus concentration trends … decomposition solubility biological". Only Indian
   catchment projections (Sci. Rep. 2022, ≈ 2.3 % DOsat loss per °C) and Guan 2026 press coverage.
10. Related, in the model check: Guan et al. 2025 ES&T (DL metabolism at 293 US rivers; NEP declining). This gives
    the metabolism side at daily resolution but does not map to DO_min sensitivity.

## Conclusion (provisional)
- **Established:**
  - temperature is the dominant DO driver at continental scale (Zhi 2023 NW);
  - widespread deoxygenation (Zhi 2023 NCC; Guan 2026 Sci. Adv.);
  - solubility explains most of the global decline (Guan 2026, press-level [U]);
  - hypoxia is nocturnal and short (Blaszczak 2023).
- **Not found:** a national, **sensor-based, diel-resolved** analysis of how *nightly minimum* DO (and D) responds to
  temperature anomalies, compared against the pure-solubility slope site by site, with departures explained by
  traits.
- Both Zhi papers explicitly flag this as future work.
- Classification: **partially novel (extension)**. The headline "warming deoxygenates rivers mostly via
  solubility" is *not* ours to claim. The diel and nightly-minimum focus and the per-site solubility benchmark are
  the new elements.
- Permitted wording: "Prior continental analyses used daytime grab samples (Zhi et al. 2023a,b) or did not resolve
  the diel cycle (Guan et al. 2026 [U]). We provide, to our knowledge, the first sensor-based national estimate
  of nightly-minimum DO sensitivity to temperature relative to solubility."
- Do not claim to be first to separate solubility from biology.

  2023 NCC, Zhi 2023 NW and Blaszczak 2023 for 2024–2026 sensor-based papers.
